## Slide 2 — What is Calisthenics?

> So first — what even is calisthenics? Basically, it's training using your own bodyweight. No dumbbells, no weight machines — just you. Push-ups, pull-ups, dips, muscle-ups...
>
> Now, that doesn't mean zero equipment — you definitely use stuff like bars, rings, parallettes. But the weight? That's always YOU. Your body is the resistance.
>
> That's what makes it different from gym or weightlifting. Nobody's asking "how much do you bench?" — it's more like "can you do a muscle-up?" or "how long can you hold a front lever?"
>
> And the nice thing is, you can do it pretty much anywhere there's a bar. Parks, home, a random playground... you figure it out.

---

## Slide 3 — My Journey

> So my journey started in 2019. I discovered it kind of randomly — started doing push-ups in my room, then I found a park nearby with bars, and I got hooked.
>
> But I wasn't always consistent... When I started faculty, I stopped a few times. You know how it is — exams, laziness, "I'll start again next week"... classic.
>
> *(smile)* At one point I even tried making content on Instagram. Posted a few training videos, thought I was going to become a fitness influencer or something... Yeah, that lasted about two weeks. Deleted everything. No regrets though — it was funny while it lasted.

---

## Slide 4 — How I Train

> At the beginning, the best part was training with friends in the park. That group energy is something else — you're competing, spotting each other, trying new moves, laughing when you fail... It's like a community.
>
> Then I moved to Casablanca, and I had to start from scratch. No crew, different city. I tried finding spots, trained alone. It's doable, but it's definitely not the same vibe.
>
> And now? *(laugh)* Now I have a multifunctional bar at home. Pull-ups in the living room. Dips between two chairs. It's not exactly "street workout" anymore... but hey, it counts.

---

## Slide 5 — Why It Matters to Me

> So why am I talking about this? Because calisthenics is genuinely a big part of my life.
>
> When I stop training for a while, I feel it. Not just physically — mentally. Something feels off. Like I'm missing a piece of my routine.
>
> It also really helps me with stress. After a long day at work, a good set of pull-ups just clears my head. Better than any app or meditation — sorry, not sorry.
>
> And honestly, it's just about balance. Feeling grounded. Feeling like I'm in control of something, even when everything else is chaotic.
>
> *(pause, then wrap up)*
>
> So yeah — that's calisthenics for me. It's simple. You don't need a fancy gym or a personal trainer. Just a bar, some discipline, and maybe a bit of stubbornness. But it has a real impact. Thanks!
